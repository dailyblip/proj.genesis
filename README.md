# Genesis

**Doctrine reset: 2026-08-24**

Genesis is one experiment: determine whether an AI-operated system can earn one voluntary payment from a stranger, starting from zero audience.

Older actors, bounty workers, and social-content files are historical artifacts. They are not active experiments and must not be restarted under this doctrine.

## Before starting

State what is deliberately not being built and why the simpler version is sufficient.

## Experiment

Test whether an AI-operated system can earn one voluntary payment from a stranger, starting from zero audience.

The hypothesis is that the answer is something obvious and unimpressive. A clever or novel product does not test this hypothesis better—it tests it worse, because sophistication introduces confounds. If the product starts becoming interesting, Genesis has drifted.

## Success

One person the operator has never met, who found the product without any promotion from the operator, pays for it once.

## Hard constraints on the product

- Buildable in under 4 hours
- No backend, database, user accounts, authentication, or ongoing compute
- Fully described by its listing title; if the title needs a subtitle to make sense, it is too complicated
- Solves exactly one annoyance, for one kind of person, once
- Delivered as a file or static page; nothing that runs
- Priced from $3 to $15; do not optimize the price

## Forbidden

- Bounty boards, task marketplaces, faucets, airdrops, or anything where the transaction is the product
- Anything requiring the operator to post, promote, or have an audience
- Any category where demand must be invented
- Any spam-adjacent tactic that technically converts

## Method

Follow these steps in order. Do not skip ahead or reopen a closed decision.

1. Find three pieces of quoted evidence that a specific want already exists: a repeated question, recurring complaint, search term with volume, or a thing people currently do by hand. Inference does not count. If three pieces cannot be found, the category dies.
2. Pick one marketplace that already has organic traffic for that category. Confirm comparable products there have sales.
3. Commit in writing to the category and marketplace. The decision is closed and may not be reopened.
4. Build the artifact, write the listing, and publish it.
5. Watch. Do not build anything new.

## Required output

For each step, report only:

- **Decision**
- **Evidence**
- **Single biggest reason it might fail**

No plans, roadmaps, or phases.

## Kill criterion

Fourteen days after publication with no sale, write a postmortem naming the one thing that was wrong. Then return to step 1 with a different category. Do not iterate on a dead listing.
