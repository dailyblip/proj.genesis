# Genesis

**Doctrine reset: 2026-08-24**

Genesis tests whether an AI-operated system can earn one voluntary payment from a stranger, starting from zero audience.

The hypothesis favors something obvious and unimpressive. A clever or novel product is a worse test because sophistication introduces confounds. If the product becomes interesting, Genesis has drifted.

Older actors, bounty workers, social-content files, and killed candidates are historical artifacts only. They must not be restarted or iterated.

## Success

One person the operator has never met, who found the product without any promotion from the operator, pays for it once.

## Hard constraints

- Buildable in under 4 hours
- No backend, database, accounts, authentication, or ongoing compute
- Fully described by its own listing title
- Solves exactly one annoyance for one kind of person once
- Delivered as a file or static page; nothing runs
- Priced from $3 to $15; do not optimize the price
- One listing only: not a bundle, shop, or product line

## Forbidden

- Bounty boards, task marketplaces, faucets, airdrops, or anything where the transaction is the product
- Anything requiring the operator to post, promote, or have an audience
- Any category where demand must be invented
- Any spam-adjacent tactic that technically converts
- Any idea appearing in listicles of things to sell online; that is evidence of supply, not demand

## Before starting

State:

1. what is deliberately not being built;
2. why the simpler version is sufficient; and
3. in one sentence, the specific person who types the specific phrase.

## Step 1: Find a want

Demand must be evidenced at the specific version, not category level.

Produce three pieces of evidence that all satisfy these rules:

- quoted verbatim with a source; no inference or paraphrase;
- shows buying or hiring intent, or a task currently performed by hand;
- someone asking how to make the thing is evidence against;
- all three refer to the same specific version.

Then search the marketplace for the literal proposed title and count near-identical listings. More than approximately three pages of near-identical listings kills the category. Do not differentiate a saturated category; narrow further or start over.

Assume the listing competes from a shop with zero sales and zero reviews. Specificity is the only ranking advantage.

## Locked method

1. Find the want and pass the saturation kill test.
2. Pick the marketplace with organic traffic for that exact phrase and confirm comparable listings show actual sales.
3. Commit in writing to the product, phrase, and marketplace. The decision is closed and final.
4. Build the artifact, write the listing, and publish.
5. Watch. Build nothing new and add no listings.

## Required output

For every step, report only:

- **Decision**
- **Evidence**
- **Single biggest reason it might fail**

No plans, roadmaps, or phases.

## Kill criterion

Fourteen days after publication with no sale, write a postmortem naming the one thing that was wrong. Then return to step 1 with a different want. Do not iterate on the dead listing or add a second listing to improve shop authority.
